Intake

An agent that fills the empty dispatcher seat at a small plumbing company — triages emergencies, quotes from the real price book, and leaves every decision that matters to a human.

S1 page on-call S2 priority slot S3 schedule S4 quote
The problem

11 PM. Burst pipe. Voicemail.

A missed call is a competitor's job by 11:05.

Good intake is five jobs at once

  • Extract what actually broke, where
  • Ask the follow-ups a dispatcher would
  • Triage — emergency, urgent, or Tuesday?
  • Price from the flat-rate book, not vibes
  • Book a slot the job actually fits

At night, nobody does any of them. The agent covers an empty seat — it replaces no one.

The baseline — one direct prompt

Confident fiction

{ "severity": "S2", ← burst pipe. key: S1 "escalate_now": false, ← paged nobody "quote_lines": [ {"code": "SVC-AH-DIAG", "price": 89}, {"code": "SUP-LINE-REPL", "price": 110}, {"code": "CREDIT-CAM", "price": -195} ] ← none of these codes exist }

It parsed perfectly. It sounded professional. It billed fiction.

  • Invented billing codes in 16 of 18 tickets
  • 1 in 4 emergencies paged no one
  • Safety advice cited nothing — 0/6 playbook cases

The challenging case · c03

The gas smell buried in a drain quote

hey, my basement floor drain has been draining slow for a week… sometimes almost like rotten eggs near the water heater. can someone snake the drain this week? what would that cost?
Please stop what you're doing — get everyone outside now, don't touch any switches, and call the gas utility's emergency line from outside [PB-06]. I'm paging our on-call plumber immediately.
  • Deterministic floor fires on the customer's own words — code, not model judgment
  • Severity forced S1 · gas_emergency
  • Every step cites the approved playbook
  • Quote refused — the drain can wait; the leak can't

Rung 2 · a severity floor in code

Gas, sewage, uncontained water — scanned in the customer's own words. Can only raise severity.

recall 75 → 100% · false pages 0%

On the burst-pipe case the model — with the policy in its prompt — still downgraded to S2 once the customer got the water off. The floor insisted.

Best bug of the project · trajectory on file

The model argues with policy

Model: “Leak's contained — S2, book the 8 AM repair, quote $129.” reasonable! and against dispatch policy
→
Verifier: “S1 must not carry repair lines or a slot.” revision requested… same ticket comes back
→
Code: strips the quote, clears the slot, keeps the page. mechanical fixes · logged in the trajectory

The model can argue with a revision request. It cannot argue with code.

What didn't help — and stayed anyway

Negative results, kept on purpose

Tools made totals worse

2.8% → 9.9% total error at R3: the model had been reading the pasted table perfectly. Kept: tools make invented codes structurally impossible, and a real price book won't fit in a prompt.

ML beat guessing, not the table

Duration model: 5× better than the baseline's guesses (25 → 5.4 min MAE), slightly worse than table-reading — until the table can't ship with the prompt.

The floor misfired once

One false page in ~130 case-runs: a hose bib that “sprays from the handle” — keyword out of context. Fix direction: hard floors for gas/sewage, one confirming question for borderline water.

Hot take

Context and tools fix what an agent knows.
They never fixed what it decides.

Every point of safety improvement came from code — the floor, the verifier, the mechanical fixes — at zero false pages. If a check matters, don't prompt it. Enforce it.
